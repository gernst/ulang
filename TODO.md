## Diagnostics and refactorings

1. **Positions are only those of commands**: `ulang/syntax.u` keeps a position in `Top` only, so type errors are reported at their declaration, not at the expression, and no use of a name can be found again, which queries and renaming need. Solution: positions on the uses of names in the parsed tree, variables, the names of calls, and constructors in patterns, and the checker records, for each, the qualified name it resolved to and its type; no spans, since refactorings print the tree again (item 5). Compiler 1 can carry them on.
2. **A run reports only the first error, as a line of text**, which tools and agents parse, and the VS Code extension by a regular expression. Solution: all errors in one run, where the checker continues after an error with the next command, taking the declared signature of the failed one, and the parser resumes at the next command, a keyword in column 1; and a datatype `Diagnostic` in `ulang/error.u` with a stable code, the constructor of the error, e.g. `CannotUnify`, the file and position, and fields such as the expected and found types, the candidates with their types, or the missing patterns. It is printed as text by default, the style of GCC that editors and agents read, with indented lines `key: value`, e.g. `tests/a.u:12.7: error[CannotUnify]: cannot unify Int and Str`, `  expected: Int`, and by `--format=jsonl` one object per line, for the extension. Success prints nothing or one line, and the exit status tells parse, type, test, and internal errors apart. Tests of errors state their code and position, `// error[CannotUnify] at 3.1`, independent of the wording of messages.
3. **Errors do not suggest their fix** where the checker knows it. Solution: suggested edits as refactorings (item 5), e.g. the missing cases of a match, or a qualified name for an ambiguous use, listed with the diagnostic and carried out by `--apply`.
4. **The resolution of names is invisible**, and finding the uses of a declaration needs reading, since `size` in a file that imports `set` and `map` may be any of three. Solution, on top of item 1: `ulang.bin resolve <file> <L.C>`, the qualified name, type, and declaration of the use there, also as hover in the VS Code extension; `ulang.bin refs` of a use or of `module.name/arity`, all its uses in the files that import it; `ulang.bin sig <module>`, the exported signatures without bodies; and `check` of several files or a directory in one process.
5. **No refactorings**, so renaming a declaration means finding its uses by hand. Solution: refactorings as changes of the parsed tree, printed by `format_program`, which keeps docs since they are syntax: refused unless `format` reproduces the file before (Self-hosted toolchain 7); checked again after; and refused if any other use resolves differently from before, which makes them safe under overloading without reasoning about it. They print a diff, or write the files with `--apply`. In order: rename, filling in the missing cases of a match, adding a field to a constructor with its constructions and patterns, and changing a signature with its calls; not extracting or inlining, which edits of the text do well enough.
6. **Code that could be simpler is only found by reading it**, e.g. `list(for e in list(for x in m) …)` instead of `for k => v in m`, the same projection `list(for x in xs yield x.name)` repeated, a hand-written `match` on `get(…)` that is `value_or`, or a collecting loop `xs := xs ++ list(v)` instead of a `Buffer`. Solution: rewrites on the checked syntax tree, each from a pattern to a simpler term that keeps the meaning, which `ulang.bin check` reports as warnings with the position and the replacement, which `--apply` carries out as a refactoring, and which the compiler may apply as the optimizations of Self-hosted toolchain 1; each rewrite has a test of the source that triggers it and of the result.

## Overloading

1. **Generic code over containers is impossible**, e.g. `function f[C](c: C) := size(c)`, nothing states what `size` must satisfy, and the hooks `compare`, `==`, and `show` are unchecked, with `Unordered` and `Incomparable` at run time. Deferred: named signatures, implemented by modules (`implements container with C[A] := Set[A]`), modules with parameters instantiated at the import (`import stats[set]`), one canonical implementation per type, and signatures `eq`, `ord`, and `show` that replace the hooks, where a missing implementation is a type error. Also open: laws as items of signatures, discharged once per implementing module. Until then, the hooks stay.

## Checker

1. **Failure is not in the types**: a procedure can raise any value without saying so, so a caller cannot see what may fail, and the checker cannot tell whether the handlers of a `try` are exhaustive. Solution: `raises E, …` on procedures, required in `export` and inferred inside a module, of declared datatypes only, by convention one error datatype per module. Every `raise`, and every call that can raise, is covered by a `try` or by the `raises` of the caller, the handlers are checked for exhaustiveness over the declared types, and procedure types carry their `raises` as written, without polymorphism over sets of exceptions. Open: how exceptions of functions outside their `requires` (Verification 1) count for the `raises` of a procedure, and a way to turn a raise into a value, instead of the pattern `failed: Option[TypeError]` written three times in `ulang/`, e.g. a `try` expression.
2. **Termination is a style rule only**, so the split between functions and procedures guarantees nothing, and unfolding a function that does not terminate can prove false. Solution: the checker accepts recursion in functions only if it is structural on a parameter of a datatype, or decreases a measure declared by `decreases` after `:=`, whose decrease is an obligation of the prover, and otherwise reports an error that advises a procedure.
3. **Local `let`s are generalized**, which is rarely used and complicates inference once types carry more, such as `raises`. Solution: generalize only top-level declarations, which carry their types anyway.

## Syntax

1. **Guards do not bind**, so code tests `!= None` and then matches again, with a case that cannot happen. Solution: conditions and guards that match a pattern and bind its variables in the branch they guard, e.g. `if let`.
2. **`fold` and the bounded quantifiers are missing**: a loop that accumulates is written as `foldl(c, acc, lambda …)`, and `exists` and `forall` of the containers are functions that clash with the quantifiers of specifications (`prove.md`, open point 15), which stop early (Self-hosted toolchain 8). Deferred, not convinced yet: `fold(let n := 0; for x in xs if p(x) yield n + 1)`, where `let` marks the binding of the accumulator, every `yield` and the initial value have the same type, the result is the final value, and the form runs to the end, with `foldl` and `foldr` of the modules as its translation; to consider: `fold` without parentheses, as the quantifiers are written, or the quantifiers with parentheses, as the forms are. Likewise bounded quantifiers `forall x in c => e` and `exists x in c if q => e` with the generators of the forms, where the unbounded `forall x: T => e` occurs only in specifications, which read the bounded ones as `forall x => contains(x, c) ==> e`; the functions `exists` and `forall` of the containers stay until then.
3. **Deep patterns are repeated literally**: `prove/simp.u` and `prove/syntax.u` match `EApp(EFun(Name(None, "and")), list(a, b))` and the like in dozens of cases, where a misspelt string is a valid pattern that never matches, a change of the representation is an edit at every case, and the constructors and checks in `ulang/check.u` and `ulang/compile.u` do the same on a smaller scale. Solution: pattern synonyms as macros, `pattern And(a: Expr, b: Expr): Expr := EApp(EFun(Name(None, "and")), list(a, b))`, with a doc like other commands:
   - The formals are exactly the variables of the right side, each once; the signature goes into `export` and is repeated in `implementation`, as for functions, and a synonym shares the namespace of constructors, so that clashes with them are errors.
   - A use `And(x, y)` in a pattern is expanded by the checker when it resolves the name, so that exhaustiveness, `ulang/run.u`, and `ulang/compile.u` see only constructors, exhaustiveness stays exact without declarations of complete sets, and the backends are unchanged; a synonym without `_` is also an expression, by `pattern_expr` of `ulang/syntax.u`, so `And(x, y)` builds the term too, while one with `_` is a pattern only.
   - `format` keeps the synonym at its uses, and errors of exhaustiveness name the synonym with its expansion.
   - To decide: whether a synonym over an abstract type may be exported, which would reveal its constructors to the exhaustiveness check of other modules; rather not.
   - No view patterns, `f -> p`, which call a function, defeat exhaustiveness, and need not terminate. With Self-hosted toolchain 5, the synonyms are where the names of the prover are spelt once.

## Library

1. **No binary data**: strings are code points, and the procedures of `os` read and write UTF-8 only, so a file that is not UTF-8 cannot be read. Solution: a type `Byte` with `Array[Byte]`, and procedures of `os` that read and write bytes, with explicit encodings between them and strings.

## Compiler

1. **Runtime errors have no positions.** Solution: positions in the messages of the runtime and of the interpreter, first for uncaught exceptions and failed `assert`s, from the positions of Diagnostics 1.
2. **Deep recursion**: functions like `concat` over long lists recurse deeply, so compiled programs need `ulimit -s unlimited` for big inputs. Solution: a large stack for the main thread of compiled programs, as the interpreter has.
3. **Programs need a C compiler**, and `cc -O2` takes most of the time of `make selfcompile`. Solution: a backend that writes assembly for x86-64, see `assembly.md`.

## Verification

1. **Contracts, lemmas, and proofs** are not implemented, see `prove.md`, where functions have only `requires`, which states their domain, also that of their `raise`s and of the builtins, e.g. `requires d != 0` of `/`. Solution: parse specifications, lemmas, and proofs, and prove them with the simplifier of `prove/`.
2. **The simplifier of `prove/` reads its own syntax**, by `prove/read.u`, and does not see checked programs. Solution: a translation `ulang/prove.u` from the programs checked by `ulang/check.u`, with goals as `Bool` functions whose formals are the fixed variables, a default proof by simplification and induction on each formal of a datatype, where goals proven earlier are rules for later ones, and a command `ulang.bin prove <file>`, which needs the prover in `ulang/` or the imports across directories of Modules 1, undecided.
3. **Defects of the prover**: `prove/read.u` parses the result type of a function and drops it; `merge` gets fresh fuel inside a cycle, from `add_ineq` with 10 through `add_pair` and `readd_ineqs`, so it is not bounded; and recursion is detected only for direct calls of a function to itself, so mutually recursive functions are unfolded as if they were not recursive, up to depth 64. Solution: keep the result type, one fuel for the cycle, and recursion by the strongly connected components of the calls.
4. **Fuel and limits of the prover are numbers spread over the code**, 3, 5, 10, 20, 60, and 64, and the context is built positionally, as `Ctx(…, false, 3, 0, 2)`. Solution: a record of named limits in one place.
5. **How strongly an obligation is established is not recorded.** Ideas, which need a design of their own: a status per obligation, proven, tested (Testing 1), or open; trusted facts by `assume … why "…"`; a command `ulang.bin audit <file>` that lists the assumptions and the obligations that depend on them; and runtime checks of the obligations that are not proven.

## Later

1. **Procedures may do anything that `os` offers**, invisibly in their signatures. Solution: a clause `uses os` on exported procedures.
2. **Failures of programs that read files or arguments are hard to reproduce.** Solution: `run` records the results of the `os_*` procedures and replays them.
3. **No native code of users.** Solution: `builtin` sections outside the library, with the native definitions linked in by `ulang.bin build` (Build 1).

## Rejected and decided

- **A faster `ulang/run.u`** by numbering the names of declarations and variables when a program is loaded, so that the environments compare integers, and **integers tagged in the pointer** in the runtime, to save the boxes of most integers: both rejected.
- **Hash consing** of all constructors and tuples in the runtime, tried and removed (`be1808d` is the state without it): 70% of the constructions of the compiler and 85% of `run.u` equal a live value, and peak memory fell by 40–57%, but it was 2.2–2.5 times slower from cache misses, a bucket, its entry, and the candidate for every construction. **Maps and sets ordered by hash** were 18% slower for the compiler and 75% for `run.u`, since hashes outside -16..255 are boxed and `Name` keys differ early anyway. Consing particular structures, such as types or the terms of `prove/`, for memory or identity is still open.
- **A stack machine** for the interpreter, whose stack and code would be lists or maps, and **mutable arrays**, which values shared by two variables, atomic effects, and the restoring of variables by `try` exclude: rejected.
- **Partial field accesses** marked at the access, as `e!f`, or rejected statically: dropped, `MissingField` stays.
- **Refutable patterns in generators**, such as `for Some(x) in xs`: rejected, both skipping the elements that do not match, as in Haskell, which hides a filter in the pattern and silently drops elements whose shape a mistake changed, and failing at run time, which makes a generator partial. Generators bind only irrefutable patterns (the forms), and a filter is a guard, `for o in xs if o != None`, a guard that binds (Syntax 1), or a `match` in the element.
- **`call`, `assign`, and `skip`** stay, **`in`** stays at calls, **`test name()`** keeps its parentheses, and **`[…]`** stays for type arguments, but not for lists (the forms).
- **User-defined notations** stay, although parsing depends on the imports.
- **`++` of arrays** is deferred, since arrays are built by forms.
- For the forms: no **`break`**, since a `for` runs to the end and an early exit goes through `while` or an exception; no **`Seq` type** or comprehensions without a form, since the form says what is built; no **choice of the result by the expected type**, the problem of Scala's `CanBuildFrom`, which violates locality; and no **`let` between generators** until code needs it.
- **Chains of conditions**, by `elif` or by a `when` with arms `if c => …`, tried and removed: `if` always nests, and an `if` after `else` is an `if` in its body, with its own `end`.
- **`Char`** stays a type of its own.
- **Functions may raise**, where `requires` states their domain (Verification 1).
- **Subpackages private to their parent, with re-exports** from the parent, as in Rust (`pub use`): rejected, since importing a subpackage from elsewhere is sometimes useful, and a re-export is a second way to reach the same declarations, the one transitive import, after which an import line no longer names the package that its names come from (Modules 1).
- **Overloading by type within a module**: tried and removed. Within a module, functions overload only by their number of arguments, and by type only across modules, which qualify their names, also across the files of a package (Modules 1). Telling apart the declarations of one name and arity in one module took an encoding of their types into their names, `f{(T) -> R}`, undone wherever names were compared, since a function is known by its module, name, and arity in `Sig`, `Defs`, and both backends; two hooks of one name, such as the shows of two datatypes, go into two modules.

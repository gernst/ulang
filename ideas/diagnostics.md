# Diagnostics

1. **What does not parse is kept only as tokens**: the text of a part that does not parse, `AUnparsed` of `ulang/syntax/syntax.u`, is its tokens joined as the source usually spaces them (`joined` of `ulang/parse/parse.u`), which `format` prints for an example that does not parse. Solution: the text cut from the source by the offsets of the tokens, for the language server of item 7 and the refactorings of item 4.
2. **Hints are text**: the errors give the form to write after `; ` of their message for the habits of other languages that the parser and the checker recognize, but an agent or the VS Code extension cannot carry it out, and the missing cases of a match are given one at a time, by the witness of `NotExhaustive`. Solution: suggested edits as refactorings (item 4), e.g. all the missing cases of a match, or an annotation for an ambiguous use, listed with the diagnostic as edits of item 4 and carried out by `refactor @fix`, as the edits of the lints of item 5 are.
3. **The table of resolutions misses parts**: `ulang.sh hints` (`hints` of `ulang/check/hints.u`) prints the table of the findings of one check, the resolution and type of each use, also of a pattern alias in a pattern and of the accessor or updater of a field at its name, the type of each declared variable, and the inferred raises, which the VS Code extension shows as hover and inlay hints, and which `refactor` asks by `@resolve`, `@refs`, and `@sig` (`resolve`, `refs`, `name_sig`, and `module_sig` of `ulang/refactor/refactor.u`); but the table has no declaration of a use, so definition in the editor needs a search, the types of procedure values at their calls are missing, and so are the uses of types at their names, in annotations, signatures, and datatypes, which a rename of types needs. Solution: the declaration of each use in the table, its file and span, for definition in the editor and in the server of item 7; the types of procedure values at their calls; the uses of types at their names, as written; and the queries as commands of `ulang.sh` too, without a script, `resolve <file> <L.C>`, `refs`, and `sig <module>`, if the editor or the server needs them apart from `hints`.
4. **The refactorings beyond the edit scripts are missing**: `ulang.sh refactor` (Refactoring of `README.md`) writes declarations and nodes by scripts, renames functions, procedures, pattern aliases, and constructors, moves exports, and applies the edits of the lints by `@fix`, but fills in no missing cases of a match, adds no field to a constructor with its constructions and patterns, changes no signature with its calls, and applies no edits of errors (item 2). Solution: the operations of `ideas/refactor.md`, each refused if any other use resolves differently from before, as `@rename` is; not extracting or inlining, which edits of the text do well enough.
5. **The lints know only the syntax**: `ulang.sh check --lint` (`ulang/lint/lint.u`) rewrites the parsed module, so its patterns cannot ask for types or resolved names, `Some` and `value_or` are those of the library only by convention, a list of a list, `List(for x in xs)`, is not found, the same projection `List(for x in xs yield x.name)` repeated is no rewrite of one expression, and a rule gives its fix in one of two ways, one expression as `replacement` of `Warning` of `ulang/lint/lint.u` or an `Edit` of `ulang/syntax/syntax.u`. Solution: lints that look up the resolved name and the type of a part of the parsed module by its position in the checked program, which keeps the positions but not the forms, which it translates to folds, nor the source that `format` prints; each rule gives its finding as an `Edit` instead of the one expression of `replacement`, from the same match, so that the place found and the edit stay together, while the linter never applies them: `refactor @fix` does, with the re-check of every edit; a lint is kept only if the module checks with its edit; and the compiler may apply the rewrites as the optimizations of Compiler 3. The rules that need the e-graph of the prover are planned in `ideas/lint.md`. Still open among the walks: a `match` of one irrefutable case, which is a `let`; a `case _` where the match names constructors that a new one would need a decision beside, which no criterion of the syntax tells apart from the catch-alls that the rule of style keeps, neither the number of constructors that it stands for nor a recursion of the function, since every `case _` of the project is one that the rule keeps, so that the lint waits for the line `// lint allows …` below and then proposes `expand-wildcard`; an `if contains(k, m)` before a `get(k, m)`, one `match` on `get`, which the sources have no instance of yet; appends in loops elsewhere than to an emptied list, which suggest a `Buffer` and need the types, since `++` of buffers looks the same; a doc repeated on the signature and the definition of an exported name, which the second mail client of 15 November 2026 left twice after exporting by hand, before `@export` moved the doc; an access `e.f` where the type of `e` has constructors without `f` and no enclosing `match` narrows it, such as `xs.head` of `List()`, which raises `MissingField` at run time, counted over the project before it is proposed, since `ideas/rejected.md` keeps the partial accesses; and the formals of private declarations that are never read, which cannot be `_`, since a function passed as a value keeps its arity. And a warning cannot be suppressed: a line in the doc of a command or `case`, `// lint allows ShowName: …` with a reason, that is itself a warning where it suppresses nothing, for a place such as `show_in` of `ulang/run/run.u`, `utf8`, a standard name whose digit is no index, which `NumberedName` warns on, and `show_fun`, the function of a show hook, which `ShowName` warns on, and the rules that `project.u` turns off below a directory, for the unread locals that tests write on purpose.
6. **Type errors that are follow-ups or name the checker's state**:
    - `unknown type Conn`, where `Conn` is declared in a sibling without sections, which exports nothing, does not say so, which an agent had read and forgot (the mail client of 15 November 2026), a hint that needs the private declarations of the other members, which the check of a module does not look at;
    - the messages of raises, `WiderRaise` and `UntypedRaise`, have no cases that show that they name the value and the place to change, so agents patch them by trial (the design review of 5cd2fe1).

    Solution: for an unknown name that a sibling declares privately, or in a file without sections, the hint that it is private to that file; and cases of the messages of raises.
7. **No language server**: the VS Code extension runs `ulang.bin` per action and reads its JSON lines. Solution: `ulang.bin lsp`, a server in ulang over the packages `parse` and `check`, compiled, behind a thin client in `vscode/`: diagnostics and formatting first, then the edits of item 2 as quick fixes, inlay hints of the inferred `raises` and the types of locals, and definition, hover, references, and rename from the resolutions of item 3, completion last. It reads the framing of JSON-RPC from `io_stdin`, the header lines by `io_read_line` and the body of `Content-Length` bytes by `io_read_bytes`, and needs columns converted between code points and the UTF-16 units of LSP, and checking cached per package; parsing and checking already continue past errors, as an open file needs. The server wraps the table of item 3, so it comes after it; it then also checks unsaved buffers and keeps the checked imports, which `hints` cannot.
8. **Some errors hide every other**: a file with `||` reports that one line, and its other mistakes only after it is fixed, a round trip per file (the second mail client of 15 November 2026), while the parser resumes after its errors; and a parse error in the head of a procedure skips the whole command, so an error in its body is reported only after the first is fixed (the design review of 5cd2fe1). Solution: the lexer resumes after a bad character or token, which it gives as a token of an error, so that the parser reports it and goes on; and the parser resumes in the body of a head that does not parse.
9. **Habits without a hint, or with the wrong one** (the second mail client of 15 November 2026): `xs[0]` gets the hint of container literals, not `at(0, xs)`; `binding.first` is `UnknownFieldType` at `.first`, `the type (Int, Str) of the value of .first is not known`, which is wrong, since the type is known and tuples have no fields, without the hint `first(binding)` or a pattern; an `example` after an exported signature without `:=` gets no hint at the `:=`; `xs.map(…)` is `unknown field map of List`, without the hint to write `List(for x in xs yield …)`; and `return` in a function body has none, `function f(x: Int): Int :=` then `return x + 1` being `declaration expected but x found` at the `x`, while in a procedure body `return` gets the hint that a procedure assigns its outs, and in an expression `return` parses as a name, which then also gives `unknown variable return` at it; and `xs[0]` as the body of a function gets, after its parse error, a `Mismatch` of the result, since the body parsed as far as `xs`. Solution: hints at those places, without follow-ups of the part that the parse error cut short (the last three from the design review of 5cd2fe1), `hint_at` of `ulang/parse/parse.u` and `hint_of` of `ulang/check/message.u`, with cases in `habits` of `ulang/test/errors/parse.u` and `hints` of `ulang/test/errors/types.u`.

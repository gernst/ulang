# Library

1. **Programs of the system lack the environment, the clock, and the network**: no variables of the environment, no sockets, and no processes (Build 1), and no clock, so a mail client took its credentials from a file and its server on stdin and stdout, and another wrote no `Date` header. Solution: builtin procedures of `os` and `io` in both backends, added as `io` was, in all the places of Code 2: the `builtin` section, `native_procs` and `builtin_functions` of `ulang/compile/lower.u`, which `ulang/compile/x86.u` takes by `is_native`, the runtime and `ulang/runtime/ulang.h`, and `builtin_names` of `ulang/run/run.u`, where the interpreter calls the builtin of its host, which the seed must know, so each step is two commits: the library, the runtime, and the compilers, then `make selfcompile`, then the cases of the interpreter with the tests in `ulang/prelude/test/os/`, each with `make testc` and `make testelf`, after asking.
   - The environment: `procedure os_env(in name: Str, out value: Option[Str]) raises OsError` in `ulang/prelude/os.u`, by `getenv`, `None` for a missing variable, `OsError` for a value that is not UTF-8 and for a name with `=` or a NUL; no `setenv`, since a program changes only its own copy, which matters only for the processes that it starts, whose environment `os_spawn` of Build 1 will take as an argument. Tested by `PATH`, which `make` has, and a name that nothing sets.
   - The clock: `procedure os_time(out seconds: Int)`, the wall clock in seconds since the epoch, by `clock_gettime(CLOCK_REALTIME)`, and `procedure os_clock(out nanoseconds: Int)`, a monotonic clock for durations, by `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, both procedures since they read the system, and an `Int` holds nanoseconds as it holds anything. Over them, in ulang, without builtins: `data DateTime := case DateTime(year: Int, month: Int, day: Int, hour: Int, minute: Int, second: Int)` of UTC, `utc(seconds: Int): DateTime` by the days of the civil calendar (Hinnant's `civil_from_days`), its inverse `seconds_of(t: DateTime): Int`, `weekday(t: DateTime): Int` from 0 for Monday, and the texts `format_iso(t)`, `1970-01-01T00:00:00Z`, and `format_rfc5322(t)`, `Thu, 01 Jan 1970 00:00:00 +0000`, the `Date` of mail; examples at the epoch, before it, on 29 February 2000 and 2024, and on 1 March 1900, and the lemma `seconds_of(utc(s)) == s`. No local time, which needs the time zones of the system, so `+0000` is the zone of every date. They go into `os.u`, not a new member of the library, which `implicit_imports` of `ulang/syntax/syntax.u` would show to every module.
   - Sockets, of TCP only, as streams of `IO`, read by the procedures of files, also `io_read_line`: `procedure io_connect(in host: Str, port: Int, out io: IO) raises OsError` by `getaddrinfo`, IPv4 and IPv6, with the message of `gai_strerror`; `procedure io_listen(in host: Str, port: Int, out listener: Listener) raises OsError`, with `SO_REUSEADDR`, where host `""` is every address and port 0 one that the system chooses, which `function io_port(listener: Listener): Int` gives; and `procedure io_accept(in listener: Listener, out io: IO) raises OsError`, which waits for the next connection. `Listener` is a builtin type of its own in `ulang/prelude/io.u`, since reading a listener means nothing, ordered by its opening as streams are and shown as `<listener host:port>`, closed by `io_close_listener` or the collector, which takes a descriptor `u_listener_type` in the runtime and a constructor `VListener` in `ulang/run/run.u`. The path of a socket stream, which `io_path` and `show` give, is `host:port` of what it was connected to, or of the peer that it accepted.
   - The runtime reads a socket by its `FILE`, as a file, so that `io_read_until` stays buffered, and writes it by `send` with `MSG_NOSIGNAL`, unbuffered, as it flushes stdout after each write now: one `FILE` cannot switch from reading to writing without a seek, which sockets do not have, and a write to a peer that has closed raises `OsError` instead of killing the process by `SIGPIPE`, without ignoring the signal for the whole program, which would change what `ulang … | head` does. So the stream of `ulang/runtime/ulang.h` gets the descriptor of its socket, -1 for files, and `io_close` closes both.
   - A test needs no second process: it listens on port 0 of `127.0.0.1`, connects, then accepts, since the system completes the connection before the accept, writes on one end and reads on the other, and checks that a connection to a closed port raises `OsError`.
   - Not in scope: TLS, which a mail client needs for submission, through `openssl s_client` as a process of Build 1 until a binding of a library is worth it; UDP; timeouts; and waiting for several streams at once, which a server of several clients needs, an `io_poll` over a list of streams, later, since the language has no threads. Every procedure blocks, and networking is an effect that signatures do not show (Later 1).
   - Steps: `os_env` and the clock together, as one step of two commits, then `DateTime` and its functions, which need no seed, then sockets; then `examples/http.u` (An HTTP server, 2, of `ideas/examples.md`) as the first program over them.
2. **The order of arguments is not uniform**: `take(n, xs)` and `split_at` count first, while `slice` takes the container first, `get(k, m)` and `contains(x, c)` take the element first, `repeat(s, n)` of `ulang/prelude/str.u` against `replicate(n, x)` of `ulang/prelude/list.u`, which implements it, and `join(sep, strs)` against `split(s, c)`, so an agent guesses wrong where the types agree, `Int` and `Int`, or the checker reports a mismatch far from the cause (the design review of 5cd2fe1, the review of c319a1d); and `slice` clamps for lists but is strict for strings and arrays. Solution: one rule, the container first, as `exists`, `forall`, and the folds have it, with the old orders as hints; or at least an example on each such signature.

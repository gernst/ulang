# Compiler

1. **Runtime errors have no positions.** Solution: positions in the messages of the runtime and of the interpreter, first for uncaught exceptions and failed `assert`s, from the positions that the checked program keeps (`At` of `ulang/syntax/syntax.u`).
2. **Programs need a C compiler**, and `cc -O2` takes most of the time of `make selfcompile`. Solution: a backend of x86-64, steps 0 to 9 done, see `ideas/assembly.md`, which writes assembly for `as` or ELF objects for the linker alone, while `make` and `make selfcompile` still go through C and `cc -O2`, until the assembly build is as fast, as The path there decides.
3. **No optimizations.** Solution: passes between checking and the backends, e.g. inlining small functions, folding constants, the rewrites of Diagnostics 5, and loops for folds of comprehensions beyond those over lists and arrays, which both backends fuse also with several generators (`fusion_of` of `ulang/compile/lower.u`), such as those of sets and maps.
4. **The compiler allocates about 3.8 GB to compile itself to `.o`**, against at most about 280 MB in use, so that the collector takes a third of the time even with the divisor of `ideas/assembly.md`, step 9. Counted by the callers of the allocators of the runtime in the call graph of gprof, the compiler built from its C with `-pg` and the runtime with `-fno-inline`, on 700a968: 37 million constructors, about 1.8 GB at 48 bytes a cell of a list, 9.8 million `Int`s above 255 of 32 bytes each, 4.8 million closures, and 3.1 million tuples. Of the constructors, 12 % are the cells of comprehensions fused into loops, 10 % `++` of lists, 8 % the steps of folds in lambdas, 7 % `b ++ List(x)` of buffers, 6 % the nodes of maps, 4 % `chars`, 4 % the lexer, and 3 % `le` of `ulang/compile/elf.u`. Solution, each measured by the bytes allocated in all, which gdb reads by `GC_get_total_bytes` at the exit, since the times vary with the load:
   - Done: `mangle` of `ulang/compile/lower.u` made about 3 cells per character, by `chars`, a list per character, the comprehension, and `str`, about 140,000 times per compile, since the backends mangle a name at each use; it escapes the runs of the text now (`runs`), and the compile to `.o` allocates 3,794 MB instead of 3,948 MB, on dea9109 with the same C and object. Each name mangled once per program would save the rest.
   - `zonk_type`, `subst`, and `solved_type` of `ulang/check/check.u` and `exprs_flow` of `ulang/check/raises.u` rebuild their results by comprehensions also where nothing changes: the value itself where no variable in it is substituted, if that is the common case, which is not counted yet.
   - `b ++ List(x)` of a buffer makes the list of one element and then the cell of the buffer: a rewrite of the optimizations of item 3 to the cell alone. A function `append(b, x)` of buffers saved 82 MB of 3,921 MB, but was reverted, since it is a second way to write the same, an agent writing `append(xs, x)` of a list then gets no candidate instead of the hint of `xs ++ ys` (`foreign_names` of `ulang/check/check.u`), and the guess fits only buffers.
   - The `Int`s above 255 (`SMALL_MAX` of `ulang/runtime/ulang.c`), about 310 MB, among them every offset of the encoder and positions past column 255: unboxed small integers, `ideas/assembly.md`, step 10.
   - The mark stack of the collector overflows about 2,200 times per compile to `.o` and 860 to `.s`, each a rescan of the heap, since a cell of a list is marked by pushing its head before its tail, so that the heads of a long list of objects in the heap wait on the stack: in `assemble_program` of `ulang/compile/x86.u`, `encode_lines` and `encode_object` of `ulang/compile/elf.u`, and `lex_member` of `ulang/parse/parse.u`, found in gdb at each overflow, and in a test program a list of a million pairs overflowed 15 times as often as an array of them. A mark procedure for constructors that pushes their fields from the last, by `GC_new_proc` of libgc, would keep it shallow; libgc has no size of the mark stack to set.

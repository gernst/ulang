# Compiler

1. **Runtime errors have no positions**, nor a trace: `uncaught exception DivisionByZero` of a mail client named neither the line nor the procedure, a search in a larger program. Solution: positions in the messages of the runtime and of the interpreter, first for uncaught exceptions and failed `assert`s, from the positions that the checked program keeps (`At` of `ulang/syntax/syntax.u`).
2. **The assembly backend is about as simple as it can be**, so that `make` and `make selfcompile` still go through C and `cc -O2`, which takes most of their time, until the assembly build is as fast (`CLAUDE.md`, Build): on the sources of 5ad5ed1, the tool took 31.8 s from C and 10.7 s from `.o`, but the simple backend, every value in a slot of the frame, is about 1.1 times slower than `-O2` on the compiler and about twice on the suite, and `make test` took 83 s by the C build and 89 s by the build of the `.o`. Solution, each a project of its own: register allocation for locals and temporaries, after which `make` switches to the object; then ARM64, a second instruction set and convention, and possibly a JIT, whose encoder `ulang/compile/elf.u` already is. First measure the improvements made without registers, which were not timed since the machine was loaded: the fusion of comprehensions (`comprehension_loops` of `ulang/compile/x86.u`), the last argument of a call kept in `%rax` and arguments loaded directly into their registers (`compile_args` and `operand_of`), and single assignments and scrutinees of a local without a slot of their own (`compile_slot`), which shrank the `.s` of `compile --tests` from 553,000 to 455,000 lines.
3. **No optimizations.** Solution: passes between checking and the backends, e.g. unboxed small integers, inlining small functions, folding constants, the rewrites of Diagnostics 5, and loops for folds of comprehensions beyond those over lists and arrays, which both backends fuse also with several generators (`fusion_of` of `ulang/compile/lower.u`), such as those of sets and maps.
4. **The compiler allocates about 3.8 GB to compile itself to `.o`**, against at most about 280 MB in use, so that the collector takes a third of the time even with the divisor 1 that the runtime sets (`CLAUDE.md`, Build). Counted by the callers of the allocators of the runtime in the call graph of gprof, the compiler built from its C with `-pg` and the runtime with `-fno-inline`, on 700a968: 37 million constructors, about 1.8 GB at 48 bytes a cell of a list, 9.8 million `Int`s above 255 of 32 bytes each, 4.8 million closures, and 3.1 million tuples. Of the constructors, 12 % are the cells of comprehensions fused into loops, 10 % `++` of lists, 8 % the steps of folds in lambdas, 7 % `b ++ List(x)` of buffers, 6 % the nodes of maps, 4 % `chars`, 4 % the lexer, and 3 % `le` of `ulang/compile/elf.u`. Solution, each measured by the bytes allocated in all, which gdb reads by `GC_get_total_bytes` at the exit, since the times vary with the load:
   - `mangle` of `ulang/compile/lower.u` runs about 140,000 times per compile, since the backends mangle a name at each use: each name mangled once per program.
   - `zonk_type`, `subst`, and `solved_type` of `ulang/check/check.u` and `exprs_flow` of `ulang/check/raises.u` rebuild their results by comprehensions also where nothing changes: the value itself where no variable in it is substituted, if that is the common case, which is not counted yet.
   - `b ++ List(x)` of a buffer makes the list of one element and then the cell of the buffer: a rewrite of the optimizations of item 3 to the cell alone. A function `append(b, x)` of buffers saved 82 MB of 3,921 MB, but was reverted, since it is a second way to write the same, an agent writing `append(xs, x)` of a list then gets no candidate instead of the hint of `xs ++ ys` (`foreign_names` of `ulang/check/check.u`), and the guess fits only buffers.
   - The `Int`s above 255 (`SMALL_MAX` of `ulang/runtime/ulang.c`), about 310 MB, among them every offset of the encoder and positions past column 255: unboxed small integers, item 3.
   - The mark stack of the collector overflows about 2,200 times per compile to `.o` and 860 to `.s`, each a rescan of the heap, since a cell of a list is marked by pushing its head before its tail, so that the heads of a long list of objects in the heap wait on the stack: in `assemble_program` of `ulang/compile/x86.u`, `encode_lines` and `encode_object` of `ulang/compile/elf.u`, and `lex_member` of `ulang/parse/parse.u`, found in gdb at each overflow, and in a test program a list of a million pairs overflowed 15 times as often as an array of them. A mark procedure for constructors that pushes their fields from the last, by `GC_new_proc` of libgc, would keep it shallow; libgc has no size of the mark stack to set.
5. **Errors of the assembly backend show as crashes**, without a hint at the statement whose code is wrong. Solution: each statement preceded by an `LComment` of `ulang/compile/asm.u` naming it, which `compile_stmt` of `ulang/compile/x86.u` does not emit yet, so that the `.s` of a crash tells the statement; if that is not enough, an interpreter of `AsmFile` in ulang, with the runtime calls it needs, which reports a fault at its instruction.
6. **The C of the suite has warnings**: `make testc` builds `out/suite/suite.c` with `-Wall` but `-Wno-unused-variable` and `-Wno-unused-function`, and clang still reports 22 times `variable … set but not used`, all in tests, of locals that a test assigns and never reads: the `(value)` of an `assert raises` whose body computes a value only for its exception, the `(depth)` of the generated tests of lemmas (`ulang/test/generate.u`), and locals of tests such as `r`, `c`, and `b`, so that a warning of a real defect in the generated C would drown among them. Solution: no declaration of a local that nothing reads, by an analysis of `ulang/compile/compile.u` or by the checker, which also drops the assignments of its values but their effects; then `-Wall` without exceptions, as far as it goes, perhaps `-Werror` in `make testc`.

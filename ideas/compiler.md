# Compiler

1. **Runtime errors have no positions**, nor a trace: `uncaught exception DivisionByZero` of a mail client named neither the line nor the procedure, a search in a larger program. Solution: positions in the messages of the runtime and of the interpreter, first for uncaught exceptions and failed `assert`s, from the positions that the checked program keeps (`At` of `ulang/syntax/syntax.u`).
2. **The assembly backend is about as simple as it can be**, so that `make` and `make selfcompile` still go through C and `cc -O2`, which takes most of their time, until the assembly build is as fast (`CLAUDE.md`, Build): on the sources of 5ad5ed1, the tool took 31.8 s from C and 10.7 s from `.o`, but the simple backend, every value in a slot of the frame, is about 1.1 times slower than `-O2` on the compiler and about twice on the suite, and `make test` took 83 s by the C build and 89 s by the build of the `.o`. Solution, each a project of its own: register allocation for locals and temporaries, after which `make` switches to the object; then ARM64, a second instruction set and convention, and possibly a JIT, whose encoder `ulang/compile/elf.u` already is. First measure the improvements made without registers, which were not timed since the machine was loaded: the fusion of comprehensions (`comprehension_loops` of `ulang/compile/x86.u`), the last argument of a call kept in `%rax` and arguments loaded directly into their registers (`compile_args` and `operand_of`), and single assignments and scrutinees of a local without a slot of their own (`compile_slot`), which shrank the `.s` of `compile --tests` from 553,000 to 455,000 lines.
3. **No optimizations.** Solution: passes between checking and the backends, e.g. inlining small functions, folding constants, the rewrites of Diagnostics 5, and loops for folds of comprehensions beyond those over lists and arrays, which both backends fuse also with several generators (`fusion_of` of `ulang/compile/lower.u`), such as those of sets and maps.
4. **The compiler allocates about 3.8 GB to compile itself to `.o`**, against at most about 280 MB in use, so that the collector takes a third of the time even with the divisor 1 that the runtime sets (`CLAUDE.md`, Build). Counted by the callers of the allocators of the runtime in the call graph of gprof, the compiler built from its C with `-pg` and the runtime with `-fno-inline`, on 700a968: 37 million constructors, about 1.8 GB at 48 bytes a cell of a list, 9.8 million `Int`s above 255 of 32 bytes each, which are tagged words since ae62058, 4.8 million closures, and 3.1 million tuples. Of the constructors, 12 % are the cells of comprehensions fused into loops, 10 % `++` of lists, 8 % the steps of folds in lambdas, 7 % `b ++ List(x)` of buffers, 6 % the nodes of maps, 4 % `chars`, 4 % the lexer, and 3 % `le` of `ulang/compile/elf.u`. Solution, each measured by the bytes allocated in all, which gdb reads by `GC_get_total_bytes` at the exit, since the times vary with the load:
   - `mangle` of `ulang/compile/lower.u` runs about 140,000 times per compile, since the backends mangle a name at each use: each name mangled once per program.
   - `zonk_type`, `subst`, and `solved_type` of `ulang/check/check.u` and `exprs_flow` of `ulang/check/raises.u` rebuild their results by comprehensions also where nothing changes: the value itself where no variable in it is substituted, if that is the common case, which is not counted yet.
   - `b ++ List(x)` of a buffer makes the list of one element and then the cell of the buffer: a rewrite of the optimizations of item 3 to the cell alone. A function `append(b, x)` of buffers saved 82 MB of 3,921 MB, but was reverted, since it is a second way to write the same, an agent writing `append(xs, x)` of a list then gets no candidate instead of the hint of `xs ++ ys` (`foreign_names` of `ulang/check/check.u`), and the guess fits only buffers.
   - The mark stack of the collector overflows about 2,200 times per compile to `.o` and 860 to `.s`, each a rescan of the heap, since a cell of a list is marked by pushing its head before its tail, so that the heads of a long list of objects in the heap wait on the stack: in `assemble_program` of `ulang/compile/x86.u`, `encode_lines` and `encode_object` of `ulang/compile/elf.u`, and `lex_members` of `ulang/parse/parse.u`, found in gdb at each overflow, and in a test program a list of a million pairs overflowed 15 times as often as an array of them. A mark procedure for constructors that pushes their fields from the last, by `GC_new_proc` of libgc, would keep it shallow; libgc has no size of the mark stack to set.
5. **Errors of the assembly backend show as crashes**, without a hint at the statement whose code is wrong. Solution: each statement preceded by an `LComment` of `ulang/compile/asm.u` naming it, which `compile_stmt` of `ulang/compile/x86.u` does not emit yet, so that the `.s` of a crash tells the statement; if that is not enough, an interpreter of `AsmFile` in ulang, with the runtime calls it needs, which reports a fault at its instruction.
6. **The C of the suite has warnings**: `make testc` builds `out/suite/suite.c` with `-Wall` but `-Wno-unused-variable` and `-Wno-unused-function`, and clang still reports 22 times `variable … set but not used`, all in tests, of locals that a test assigns and never reads: the `(value)` of an `assert raises` whose body computes a value only for its exception, the `(depth)` of the generated tests of lemmas (`ulang/test/generate.u`), and locals of tests such as `r`, `c`, and `b`, so that a warning of a real defect in the generated C would drown among them. Solution: no declaration of a local that nothing reads, by an analysis of `ulang/compile/compile.u` or by the checker, which also drops the assignments of its values but their effects; then `-Wall` without exceptions, as far as it goes, perhaps `-Werror` in `make testc`.
7. **A `for` over a source other than a list, an array, or a string walks a temporary list**, `List(for (item) in c)` of `items` of `ulang/check/check.u`, since its body assigns the variables around it, which the function of a fold cannot. Solution: Syntax 5, a fold as a procedure, whose sink is the body with the variables that it assigns as the `ref` state.
8. **The encoder builds a list of `Int`s for each field before it makes the bytes an array**: `chunk_of(le(rela.offset, 8) ++ le(rela.kind, 4) ++ …)` of `ulang/compile/elf.u` makes the list of each `le` and copies it by each `++`, so that every byte of the object is a cell of a list, 48 bytes, several times, before it becomes 8 bytes of an `Array[Byte]` (Compiler 4). Solution: the fused loops of comprehensions take a generator over `xs ++ ys` as two loops, one after the other, without the joined list, and over a call of a small function that builds a list, such as `le`, by its body, as the inlining of item 3, so that `Array(for b in le(a, 8) ++ le(b, 4) yield byte(b))` fills the array directly.
9. **Every object pays a byte of padding**: without `GC_set_all_interior_pointers(0)` before `GC_INIT`, the collector adds a byte to every object, so that one of 32 bytes takes 48 and a cell of a list 64, 3 to 7 % of the instructions of ubox in an experiment of 16 November 2026. Solution: the call, safe only if no object is reached from the heap solely by a pointer into its middle, which every pointer that the runtime stores into an object must be checked for, `items`, `units`, the limbs of GMP, and those of `ulang/runtime/window.c`, a pointer that `GC_register_displacement` can cover; a spot check found slices copying and every `units` at the start of its block, which is no proof.
10. **Compiled programs stay 5 to 80 times slower than coreutils** after the runtime changes of 16 November 2026 (ubox), estimates from the profiles of the same experiment, untried: `sort` of the library builds cells at each merge and compares by the generic `compare`, about 20 % of `sort -n` in allocation, so a builtin sort that copies the list into an array, sorts it in C by the same order, and builds the list once; a header of objects of 16 to 24 bytes instead of 32, 25 to 50 % less heap, the broadest gain but the layout that both backends and the `_Static_assert`s of `ulang/runtime/ulang.c` depend on; tail calls in `ulang/compile/x86.u`, which `cc` gives the C backend, so that `factor` built as an object runs 3 times slower than via C and deep loops overflow the stack; and a builder of strings (Library 4). Building lists in place in recursions, tail recursion modulo cons, is judged not worth its change of the compiler, and smaller initial heaps were slower, so `GC_expand_hp` of 256 MB and the divisor 1 stay.
11. **No profile of a run** (ubox, Refactor 5B5): each slow spot was found by guessing and replacing, and one guess was wrong (5B6), while gprof works only on compiled programs built for it (`CLAUDE.md`, Working rules). Solution: `ulang.sh run --profile`, the time and the number of calls of each function and procedure in the interpreter, printed at the exit.
12. **The collector traces what the program keeps and copies what it changes**: the Boehm collector takes about a third of the time of the compiler (item 4) and occasionally much longer, from the rescans after its mark stack overflows, while ulang allows more: values are immutable and built from their parts, closures capture by value, and nothing is `let rec`, so no value reaches itself and counting references frees all, without a collector of cycles; and the copies of outs and refs that a procedure writes back at its end, which make effects atomic, give a callee the value of a ref to change. GMP's limbs are allocated atomic since f5e6954, which shortened marking by about 13 %. Solution, a project of its own, by Perceus (Koka) and Lean 4: reference counts in both backends and the runtime, with drops and dups inserted by the compilers, ins borrowed or owned by an analysis per formal, outs handed over, and the reuse of a cell of a count of 1 for the value built from it, so that `e with f := a`, the rebuilding of `zonk_type` and `subst` of `ulang/check/check.u`, the insertions into maps, the updates of an `Array`, and `++` of a buffer or string with a unique left side work in place, which attacks the 3.8 GB of item 4 at its source; the heap stays near the live size, and streams close when their last reference goes.
    - Refs: the value of a ref may be moved into the callee and changed in place unless the restore after a raise is observable, which only a handler that can catch the exception of the call and reads the variable before assigning it can observe, directly or through the chain of ref formals up the callers, a fixpoint over the call graph as `infer_procs` of `ulang/check/raises.u` is; such an argument is passed shared, so that the callee copies. Most calls are free of it: of about 2,200 occurrences of `ref` in `ulang/`, few are inside one of its 148 `try`s, but the parser's recovery, `skip_command(ref toks)` in a handler after `parse_x(ref toks)`, reads exactly the value before the `try`. Builtin exceptions such as `DivisionByZero` can be caught, so a procedure that raises nothing by its raises may still unwind, unless they become uncatchable as a failed `assert` is.
    - Exceptions: `longjmp` skips the drops of the frames it unwinds, so either exceptions are compiled as results checked after each call, which also removes `setjmp` and its miscompilations (`BUGS.md`) and the saved slots of a `try`, or each handler keeps a chain of the owned slots to drop.
    - The alternative that keeps the backends: a heap with layouts in `struct u_type` and a conservative stack, generational and mostly copying (Bartlett, or Immix as in Scala Native), whose bump allocation in a nursery suits the short lives of cells of lists, and whose write barrier is almost empty, since old objects reach young ones only through the caches of functions without parameters, `u_array_push` into a promoted array, and the saved slots of handlers; it gives no reuse in place.

# Later

1. **Procedures may do anything that `os` offers**, invisibly in their signatures. Solution: a clause `uses os` on exported procedures.
2. **Failures of programs that read files or arguments are hard to reproduce.** Solution: `run` records the results of the `os_*` procedures and replays them.
3. **No native code of users**: a `builtin` section outside the library checks, but nothing can define its names. Solution: native definitions of them linked in by `ulang.bin build` (Build 1), and found by the interpreter.
4. **Streams are checked only at run time**: a stream of `IO` of `ulang/prelude/io.u` that is used after `io_close` or closed twice raises `OsError`, one that is never closed waits for the collector, and any procedure may open files or read `io_stdin`, which its signature does not show (item 1). Two extensions of the type system would catch more, at different costs:
   - **Linear types**, a value used exactly once, would make `io_close` consume the stream and reject a use after it, a second close, and a path that drops it open, also a path that raises. But a linear type cannot instantiate a type parameter, since generic code copies and drops its values freely, nor be an element of a `List`, `Set`, or `Map`, which copy and compare theirs, so a server could not keep its connections in a `Map`; that needs qualifiers on the type variables of Hindley-Milner and linear containers, a large change for a small gain, since a closed stream already fails loudly and the collector closes a forgotten one.
   - **Capabilities**, the authority to do I/O as a value that a procedure must be given, would show in signatures which procedures touch the system, the clause `uses os` of item 1 without an effect system: `main` receives the streams and an `Os` value, `io_open` and the `os_*` procedures take it, and `io_stdin` goes. They fit the language, whose procedures already take every input as an `in` formal, and a capability that is passed can be replaced, by a recording one for the replay of item 2 or by one over strings for tests. They say nothing about closing, and they change every call of `os` and the entry of programs.

   Solution: no linear types; the protocol of closing as a flow check of the locals of type `IO`, as the checker checks that outs are assigned on every path (`Local` of `ulang/check/check.u`): opened and not closed on a path, or used after `io_close`, first as a lint; and capabilities as the form of item 1, when it is taken up.

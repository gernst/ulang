# Syntax

1. **No bounded quantifiers**: `exists(c, p)`, `forall(c, p)`, and `find(c, p)` of the containers stop at the first element that decides, but a quantifier over a container is written as a call with a lambda. Deferred, not convinced yet, since each is one more way to write a loop: bounded quantifiers `forall x in c => e` and `exists x in c if q => e` with the generators of the forms, which specifications read as `forall x => contains(x, c) ==> e`; the unbounded `forall x: T => e` occurs only in specifications, and the parser tells it from the call `forall(c, p)` by the `(`, so the names do not clash. The functions stay until then, and folds that stop early were dropped, since `exists`, `forall`, and `find` stop without them and a search that must stop otherwise is a procedure with a `while`.
2. **The printer never breaks a chain of `++`**, however long, as `description` of `ulang/ulang.u` shows, and the `usage` of a mail client became one line of 250 columns, while a `List(…)` wider than 80 characters gets one element per line, so long texts are written as lists of lines, as the cases of `ulang/test/errors/` are, and a `let` of several long bindings is one line of 300 characters; a second mail client kept six lines longer than 120 characters after `format`, comprehensions of two generators and long `List(…)` arguments of tests, which `check` accepts. Solution: a chain of an infix operator wider than 80 characters has one operand per line, each after the operator, at the indentation of the first operand.
4. **Small gaps of the syntax**: lemmas take modes, `lemma l(in x: Int)`, while tests with formals take none, `test t(xs: List[Int])`. Solution: one form for both heads. A call of an expression and `|` in operators are rejected (`ideas/rejected.md`).
5. **No source of a form or a `for` can raise, loop, or keep state**: a form takes its elements by the `foldr` or `foldl` of the module of its source, which are functions, so an iterator of the program is a datatype with folds, either a pull iterator, a state and a step `S -> Option[(A, S)]`, which allocates an option, a pair, and a state per element, and whose fold need not terminate, or a push iterator, its own fold `(R, (A, R) -> R) -> R`, whose result type `R` is a param of the datatype, since a field cannot be polymorphic, so that one bound by `let` serves the forms of one type only; neither can raise, so the lexer collects into a `Buffer` and reads it out by `List(for t in buf)`, and the encoder joins the bytes of its fields by `++` of lists before it makes them arrays (`lex_all` of `ulang/parse/lex.u`, `elf_file` of `ulang/compile/elf.u`). Solution: a procedure as a source, a generator `procedure tokens[S](in text: Str, sink: (in Token, ref S), ref acc: S) raises LexError` that calls its sink for each element in order, written as a loop, which a form or a `for` takes where a call stands, in a statement, `let toks := Array(for t in tokens(in text))`, raising what the generator raises, and which the backends compile with a sink of their own that pushes into the container, while a `for` statement passes its body as the sink, with the variables that the body assigns as the `ref` state, which is the same since the handlers of a `try` see the variables as before it; generators compose by procedures that wrap sinks, `mapping(in g, sink, out sink_)`, which lambdas allow, since they capture procedure values. The raises of a sink that a program writes stay fixed by its type (Rejected: polymorphism over exceptions), which the sinks of the backends do not need.
8. **A keyword as a name is one error per command**: the parser reads a keyword where a name is expected as that name after its error, by a parse of the command again, and reports one error per keyword and command, `keyword prefix is not a name, at 2 places` (`parse_named` of `ulang/parse/parse.u`), but an `@put` of nine declarations whose fields and formals were named `prefix` and `suffix` still replies an error per keyword in each declaration that uses it (formerly Refactor 72). Solution: one error per keyword in a module, at its first place, with the number of places in the module.
9. **The printer keeps long lists of bindings and fields on one line** (formerly Refactor 25, from the uses of `refactor`): all bindings of a `let … in` stay on one line, 290 columns, and 400 for the 13 of a `let` of the mail client of 16 November 2026 (5T13), and all fields of a constructor, 140, while a list of twelve short strings is spread (3T4); and a comment above one field of a constructor is moved above the whole `case`, where it documents the wrong thing (3T17). Solution: `let` bindings and fields one per line past 80 columns, and the fields of a constructor a sequence of lines of `lined` of `ulang/format/format.u`, which places their comments.

# Syntax

1. **No bounded quantifiers**: `exists(c, p)`, `forall(c, p)`, and `find(c, p)` of the containers stop at the first element that decides, but a quantifier over a container is written as a call with a lambda. Deferred, not convinced yet, since each is one more way to write a loop: bounded quantifiers `forall x in c => e` and `exists x in c if q => e` with the generators of the forms, which specifications read as `forall x => contains(x, c) ==> e`; the unbounded `forall x: T => e` occurs only in specifications, and the parser tells it from the call `forall(c, p)` by the `(`, so the names do not clash. The functions stay until then, and folds that stop early were dropped, since `exists`, `forall`, and `find` stop without them and a search that must stop otherwise is a procedure with a `while`.
2. **The printer never breaks a chain of `++`**, however long, as `description` of `ulang/ulang.u` shows, and the `usage` of a mail client became one line of 250 columns, while a `List(…)` wider than 80 characters gets one element per line, so long texts are written as lists of lines, as the cases of `ulang/test/errors/` are, and a `let` of several long bindings is one line of 300 characters; a second mail client kept six lines longer than 120 characters after `format`, comprehensions of two generators and long `List(…)` arguments of tests, which `check` accepts. Solution: a chain of an infix operator wider than 80 characters has one operand per line, each after the operator, at the indentation of the first operand.
4. **Small gaps of the syntax**: lemmas take modes, `lemma l(in x: Int)`, while tests with formals take none, `test t(xs: List[Int])`; and a keyword that starts a command, such as `test`, `data`, `type`, or `pattern`, written as a local, `let test := 1`, gets its hint, `write a name such as check` (`name_for` of `ulang/parse/parse.u`), and then a follow-up error, since the parse resumes at the keyword as at a command. Solution: one form for both heads; and after reporting a keyword where a name is expected, the parser reads the keyword as that name and goes on. A call of an expression and `|` in operators are rejected (`ideas/rejected.md`).
5. **The commas of an assignment mean the opposite of those of `let`**: `let a := e, b := f(a)` binds one after another, while `a := e, b := f(a)` assigns at once and reads the `a` from before, which agents get wrong (the design review of 5cd2fe1, and the lint of Diagnostics 5 that would report it). Solution: a parallel assignment written as a tuple, `(x, y) := (b, a)`, as `let (a, b) := (b, a) in e` binds in parallel, and the commas of an assignment sequential, as those of `let`; the updates of fields, `e with a := 1, b := 2`, keep their commas, since they do not read each other. A syntax, so the sources use it one `make selfcompile` after the commit that teaches it, and the assignments of several variables in the sources that read each other are rewritten first.

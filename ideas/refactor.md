# Refactor: scripted editing of ulang files by `ulang.sh`

The open items of the command `refactor`, from its uses and from its design, and what working on them needs to know. What is built is described in Refactoring of `README.md` and in the entry of `refactor` in `CLAUDE.md`; an item that is done is deleted here. The refactorings in general are Diagnostics 4, the queries `resolve`, `refs`, `sig`, and `names` with the record of resolutions behind them Diagnostics 3.

## Working on it

- **Tests**: scripts on files before and after, one test file per area in `ulang/refactor/test/`, also of the refusals, with a case of each lint rule in `fixes` of `ulang/refactor/test/scripts.u`. `project.u` runs the directory only compiled, since each parse loads the library; a single file named to `check --test` runs interpreted, far too slowly for more than one case. Build and run them by `out/ulang.sh compile --tests ulang/refactor/test -o out/rt/rt.c`, then `cc -O1 -DULANG_LIB='"'$PWD/ulang/prelude'"' -I ulang/runtime out/rt/rt.c ulang/runtime/ulang.c -lgmp -lgc -o out/rt/rt.bin`, a copy of `bootstrap/ulang.sh` as `out/rt/rt.sh`, and `out/rt/rt.sh ulang/refactor/test`, about 25 seconds of C and 50 of tests; the real check, `checked_diagnostics` of `ulang/ulang.u`, is the driver's, so a test passes a check of its own, a procedure of a file and the overlays that reads the overlay of the file before the disk, and a test of `@fix` writes a file with an entry or exports, else `UnusedPrivate` drops its declarations.
- **The design, in the decisions that new work must keep**:
  - Few actions that write code by declaration, a fragment that names itself by name and arity, and follow-ups as diagnostics with handles, which the agent fixes by `@put` at the handles; a named operation only where the follow-ups need an analysis that agents get wrong by hand, modes, raises, moves across modules, and not merely because it would save a script.
  - A script is one transaction, applied by default, reversible by `@undo` (`journal_text` of `ulang/refactor/refactor.u`); the precondition is only that the files parse (`parsed`), where an import that fails, which the parser keeps at its line as an error node (`import_failures` of `ulang/parse/parse.u`), is no failure, since the reply lists the diagnostics that are new or gone, so errors before an edit do not hide what it changed.
  - The postcondition of a rename is the comparison of resolutions, the sequence of resolved names of each command in the order of traversal, modulo the renaming (`commit_rename` and `first_difference` of `ulang/refactor/refactor.u`), read from `hints` of `ulang/check/hints.u`, which has no uses of types and none of fields at their names.
  - The lints never write: a rule gives its edit as an `Edit` of `ulang/syntax/syntax.u` in its `Warning`, from the same match, and only `@fix` applies it (`fix` and `local_splices` of `ulang/refactor/refactor.u`); the edits of errors, planned, take the same path.
  - A change is a refactoring if it needs a choice that the code does not determine, a fix if it is the one edit that removes an error, a canonicalization for lint if the code determines it, it keeps the meaning, and it is better for every reader, and an optimization, for the compiler and never the sources, if it is only faster.
- **Timing**, for judging what a loop costs: a script of check and tests on a project of 1,100 lines takes 0.4 to 0.8 seconds, an `@put` into `ulang/check/check.u` about 2 with the checks before and after about 2.7, and a rename about 3 seconds across three small files but 25 for a name of `ulang/parse/parse.u`. A `--dry` change of a private declaration of `ulang/check/check.u` takes 6.6 seconds.
- **Uses**, whose labels the items cite: R and T, the first mail client and its replay; 2T, 2R, and 2W, the second; 3T, the third, 1,142 lines in nine files through 37 scripts against `a6975f8`, whose report is summarized in the items that cite it. All three found the loop of `@put`, follow-ups with handles, `@rename`, and `@undo` right, and left the tool only for what it could not write. 4T, the two agents of 15 November 2026 that wrote `check --overlay` and `os_remove` with every edit of a `.u` file through `refactor`, without a refusal they could not get past. 5T and 5B, the two agents of 16 November 2026 against `c5e8040`, the mail client `umail`, 519 lines in seven files through 10 scripts, and `ubox/`, 860 lines in five files through 12, whose reports are summarized in the items that cite them by the number of each trip-up, 5T11 and 5B4: both judged the loop better than the edits of mainstream languages, with the reply as a diff of diagnostics with handles its best part, and the second made 2 errors of the language in 860 lines where the first made 15 in 519.

## Open items

By priority, each the problem and the solution.

### Modules and files

1. **No move across packages** (2T, 3T): extracting a package took three scripts, `@put` of the new module, `@drop`, and the imports. Solution: `@move decl F` with its doc, tests, and examples, its signature into `export` if it was exported or is used outside, imports added where now needed and removed where unused, refused with the private names it uses that the target does not see and where the imports would form a cycle or break the layers; within a package it needs no imports. Seen again in the owners of names, where moving `format_name` from `ulang/format` to `ulang/syntax` took `@drop` and `@put`. `move-test` likewise, refused on a clash of names.

### Replies and addresses

3. **The reply does not check the importers of a changed package**: a `--dry @drop` of an export that callers in an importing package use lists no new errors, only the line `F: the exports f changed, whose importers are not checked` (`changed_exports` of `ulang/refactor/refactor.u`). Checking the importers that name a changed declaration, before and after, was built and dropped again on 16 November 2026 for its cost, 16.9 instead of 6.5 seconds for a `--dry @drop` of an export of `ulang/check/check.u` with two such importers, and far more for `ulang/syntax`, which 51 files import. Solution: after Refactor 6, the importers checked once, after the script, or by `@check importers` on request.
6. **A script is several times slower than the checks it runs** (4T): 18 actions with `@check test` on `ulang/ulang.u` took 3m22, its `--dry` 2m15, while `check --test --prove` of the file takes 59 seconds, and a one-line `@patch` of `ulang/compile/lower.u` 7 seconds; on `ulang/refactor/refactor.u` a one-line `@patch` takes about 8 seconds, a `@put` of two declarations 10, and a script of seven edits across `nodes.u` and `refactor.u` 36, of which the queries and edits take little. Solution: the checks before and after a script share what they load, `Loaded` of `ulang/parse/parse.u`, where `checked_diagnostics` of `ulang/ulang.u` starts each from nothing; each loads its imports only as far as the file sees them already (under Modules of `CLAUDE.md`), and a check before that a run would take from an earlier one is rejected (`ideas/rejected.md`).

11. **Three formats of output** (3T21): errors are JSON lines, warnings and tests plain text, and replies a third form. Solution: `--json`, the edits, the affected sites, and the diagnostics as records, one per line, as the diagnostics are, which the VS Code extension needs too, for rename on F2 and the code actions of item 14. With it, `hints` can become a query of `refactor`, `@hints F`, since both read the table of `hints` of `ulang/check/hints.u` and differ only in the front end: the action writes no handles, as an editor calls it at typing speed beside the scripts of agents, and skips the checks before and after a script, so that it costs one check of the file, as the command `hints` does now.

85. **An action whose target an earlier action of the script renamed is refused without the new name** (the work on Refactor 79): a script of `@patch ulang/refactor/refactor.u:put_anchored`, whose hunk renamed the head to `procedure put_sections(`, then `@put before ulang/refactor/refactor.u:put_anchored` of a new `put_anchored`, was refused with `ulang/refactor/refactor.u declares no put_anchored`, which is right, but leaves the agent to remember the rename. Solution: the refusal names the earlier action that removed the name, and the declaration that took its place, `declares no put_anchored, which the @patch of line 1 renamed to put_sections/6`.
86. **A `@patch` that renames its declaration names only the old one** (the same work): the reply of that `@patch` was `patched ulang/refactor/refactor.u:put_anchored/5`, while the file then declared `put_sections/6` and no `put_anchored`. Solution: `patched …:put_anchored/5, now put_sections/6`, as `@put N` of a head replies `replaced F:p/3 #4, now p/2`.

### Actions

12. **Fields, types, locals, and modules are not renamed** (R17, 2T): `@rename F:T.C.field new` is refused, and `@refs` does not take them. Solution: the record of resolutions of item 21, with the uses of types and of fields at their names; a field renamed in every constructor that has it, its accessors, updates, and the pattern variables that bind it by its name with their uses, refused where the new name hides a local; locals, formals, and type parameters with `HiddenLocal` as the guard; a package by moving its directory and rewriting the imports, refused for paths with `-` or keywords; and the references of the docs, `` `old` of `file` `` by the grammar of `checks/docs.u`, rewritten, where `@rename` now only lists them (`doc_references` of `ulang/refactor/refactor.u`).
14. **Fixes of errors and follow-ups are not applied**: the edits that leave no choice, applied by `@fix` and listed as applied, through the check by which `@fix` refuses an edit of a lint that adds an error: `write-raises` of `UndeclaredRaise` and `UntypedRaise` into the signatures and the procedure types of formals; the removal of an `UnusedImport` and of a `NeverRaised` handler; a qualified name for an `Ambiguous` use where one candidate checks; the hints of the habits of other languages (Diagnostics 2); after an `@put` of a datatype, `_` for a new field in patterns and a removed one dropped from constructions; after an `@put` of a signature, a removed formal dropped from calls whose argument has no effect, and the arguments put in the order of the modes; the examples of an `ExampleOnDefinition` moved to the signature, which took 5T four `@patch` and a `@put`, since `@patch F:name` covers both, so that the old lines occur twice, while `@put F:name` of the definition with them now moves them; and the type of a `MissingType` written, that of the out of the later call that assigns it (5T6, 5B3, Checker 3). What needs a value stays a diagnostic: a new field at a construction, a new case of a match, a new `in` at a call.
15. **A fragment with a habit of another language is only refused**: `fragment 1.3, script 4.3: …` with the hint (`parse_message` and `with_script_lines` of `ulang/refactor/refactor.u`). Solution: the corrected fragment in the reply, `corrected:  x := 2`, also for `return`, `break`, `+=`, `+` of strings, `[1, 2]`, modes out of order, a keyword as a name, and an `else if` without its own `end`, which `@retry` applies.
16. **The comparison of resolutions guards only `@rename`**: every action that writes is refused if a use outside its target resolves differently, reported as the use, the name before, and the name after, which a move maps by the old and new module.
17. **Targets and handles**: `module.name/arity` of an overload, `module.T.f` of a field, `module.T.C` of a constructor, and a local as `F:proc/local`, besides those built (`target_of` of `ulang/refactor/refactor.u`); and `gone #9 (replaced by #31)` in the reply of the edit that removed a node.
18. **Named operations, each when a use asks for it**, with tests and refusals in `ulang/refactor/test/`: `expand-wildcard` of a `case _` into the remaining constructors as alternatives, with a group alias where one fits, which a lint of Diagnostics 5 offers; `fill-cases` of what `NotExhaustive` reports, with a body from the agent; `add-formal` with an argument at every call, checked in the caller's scope, `remove-formal`, `reorder-formals`, and `change-mode`, which moves the arguments to the group of the mode; `function-to-procedure`, each call in a statement made a call into a fresh `var`, and the functions that call it inside expressions listed, or turned too by `--transitive`; `tuple-to-record` of a tuple type by its constructions and patterns found by type, as `8e97aed` did by hand; `introduce-alias` of a pattern alias replacing its instances, as `2ab6b2f` did; `rewrite` by pattern, in ulang syntax with metavariables `$x` and `$x...` and `--where` on their types, refused per site where the result does not check, sharing the matcher with the rules of lint, for the same kind of replacement in many declarations, such as the 50 string literals of the tests of `ulang/refactor/test/` rewritten to the diff form of `@patch` on 16 November 2026 by a script that generated an `@put F:test` of each changed test; `extract-procedure` of statements, with the modes and raises computed, and `inline`; and `to-case` of a failing program into a case of `ulang/test/errors/`, its exception taken from a run.
19. **Canonical forms that lint lacks**, each a rewrite with a test before and after, for `@fix`: `match c case true => a case false => b end` to `if`, `if not c then a else b` swapped, `if c then skip else s end`, `if a then b else false` to `and` and its dual, `not exists` and `forall` of a negation; `size(xs) == 0` to the empty container, `xs ++ List()`, `s ++ ""`, `e with f := e.f`, `let x := e in x`; `lambda x => f(x)` to `f` where `f` is one declaration; a `foldr` or `foldl` that only conses or inserts to the comprehension; a `try` whose only handler raises what it caught unchanged; a local whose value every path overwrites before reading; a pattern variable that binds a field under another name where the field's name is free; and, opt-in, the definitions of `implementation` in the order of `export`. Smells that lint reports without an edit, since their fix is a choice: `contains` of a list in a loop over another container, `at` or `get` of an association list in a loop, `foldr` over a list that grows with the input, and the same `get(k, m)` repeated in one body, and those of ubox (5B5), each a slow program that passed every check and test, found only by measuring (Library 2): a `++` of strings whose operand is a recursive call of the same function, a `slice(s, i, size(s))` of the rest in a recursion, and `index_of` of a string of one character, `index_of(str(List(c)), s)`. Policy, which rules are on, in `Project` of `ulang/prelude/os.u`, and the rules that are terminating, confluent, and uncontroversial enforced by `check` as `format` is.

### Concurrent edits

20. **Sessions that edit the same files at once**: a script reads, checks, and writes many files, so another session can write in between. Solution, from cheap to thorough: optimistic writes, the hash of each file read compared just before the writes, the script replayed on the new texts if one changed, under a lock `out/refactor.lock` by `flock` held only for the comparison and the writes, each file written to a temporary file and renamed, with `format` and the editor taking the same lock; actions stored as intent addressed by names, so that a replay runs them on any later version; `@rename --keep-old`, which leaves a forwarder that lint reports, for sessions still writing against the old name; `--base HEAD`, the script run on the committed version in memory and replayed on the working tree, with `--patch out.diff` of its own hunks; a merge driver `ulang.sh merge base ours theirs` for `*.u` by declarations, with the diff of declarations of P1 of `ideas/lint.md`; and the journal with the session and time of each script, the replay of an inverse where other edits followed, and `refactor log` of which changes are whose.

### Changes to ulang

21. **The record of resolutions** of Diagnostics 3, which declaration each name, field, type, and constructor resolves to, for item 12, the handles of diagnostics, and item 16.
22. **A node printed alone differs from its text in the file**: `@show` and the echo of `@patch` copy the lines of the file, but the replacements of lints, which `check --lint` writes and `@fix` splices, are printed by `format_module_expr` of `ulang/format/format.u` at column 0, without the comments inside them, and so is the code of type errors, so a replacement or a message laid out otherwise than `format` lays out the node at its place occurs nowhere in the file as a `@patch` old line, and the text that `@fix` announces is not the text that lands. Solution: since the printer is ours, a node is printed with the context that `format` gives it at its place, its indentation, the notation of its module, and the comments in its span, so that its text is the slice of the formatted file, also for an error node, which prints as its tokens.
24. **Drafting**: a hole `todo`, an expression of any type and a statement, which raises `Todo`, is an open goal for the prover, and whose uses `check --test` lists, so a new constructor is stubbed in each match by `case Scroll(_) => todo`; and an optional inline `export function f(…) := …`, so that a fragment is the whole truth of a declaration.

### Found by the agents of 16 November 2026

80. **`@put before` a signature of `export` refuses a fragment of a bare signature** (Syntax 6, 16 November 2026): `@put before ulang/compile/lower.u:fusion_of` with the fragment `function let_fold_name(): Name` and its doc replied `rejected` with `fragment 3.1, script 4.1: signatures belong to the export section`, although the anchor `fusion_of` is a signature of `export`, so its place says the section; the workaround was a `@put ulang/compile/lower.u` of a fragment that writes `export`, the signature, `implementation`, and the definition. Solution: a fragment anchored before or after a declaration takes the section of the anchor, so that a bare signature next to a signature of `export` goes there.
81. **A partial last old line of `@patch` cannot delete its line** (fixing stale tests, 16 November 2026): to delete an `assert text == "…"` of one long line after `assert text == first_fix` in `ulang/refactor/test/scripts.u:fixes/test`, the hunk `-   assert text == first_fix`, `-   assert text == "function is_pos`, `+   assert text == first_fix` was taken as the start of its last line, so the rest of the string stayed and the reply was `writes a text that does not parse: …:94.44: unexpected character`; the workaround was a script generated from the file with the whole line, about 400 characters. Solution: an old line that ends in `...`, as `-   assert text == "function is_pos...`, stands for the rest of its line, so that a hunk deletes or replaces a long line by its start.
87. **A refusal of `@patch` whose old lines skip a line of the target does not say so** (Checker 8): the hunk
   ```
   @patch ulang/check/check.u:is_proc_lambda
   -// a lambda of a procedure, which a declared type or a formal types as one of a function
   -function is_proc_lambda(e: At[Expr]): Bool :=
   -    case AOk(_, _, EProc(_, _, _, _, _)) =>
   ```
   left out the line `  match e` between the second and the third old line, and the reply was `the old lines, "…", occur in no line of the target as whole lines, and the line of the target that starts most like the first of them is ulang/check/check.u:7000, …, while some of them occur in the target, which is: …`, which names the first line, which matches, rather than the line where the run breaks. Solution: the refusal names the first old line that does not follow the one before it, `the old line 3 does not follow line 2, after which the target has "  match e"`.
88. **`@rename` of an overload refuses with resolutions to constructors as locals** (the checker fixes of 16 November 2026): after a script had added `uncovered/3` to `ulang/check/check.u` beside a private `uncovered/2`, which made the export `PartlyExported`, `@rename ulang/check/check.u:uncovered/3 missed_value` was refused with `ulang/check/check.u:196.3: the use uncovered would resolve to ulang.check.check.uncovered instead of the local Some` and `ulang/check/raises.u:50.19: the use inferred_procs would resolve to ulang.check.raises.inferred_procs instead of the local CProc`, which name a constructor as a local and a use that the rename does not touch; the rename was done by a `@patch` of the three lines. Solution: the comparison of resolutions skips the positions whose names differ before and after only by the table being built from a unit with errors, and a refusal names only uses whose text is the old name; and `@put` warns when it adds a declaration beside a private one of the same name, which here made the export partial.
89. **A `@put` of an overload by type within one module replaces and adds at once, and checks to an internal error** (Library 6, 16 November 2026): `@put ulang/prelude/str.u after ulang/prelude/str.u:index_of` of an exported `function contains(part: Str, s: Str): Bool` with its definition, beside the builtin `contains(c: Char, s: Str)` of the file, replied under `--dry` `replaced contains/2 in builtin at line 81, of another type, which @put after … does not target, added contains/2 after index_of/2` and `+ ulang/prelude/str.u: internal: unexpected an error outside the commands and imports of its module, AlreadyDefined(Owned(List("prelude", "str"), List(), "contains"))`: the signature replaced the builtin, while the definition was added after the target, and the `AlreadyDefined` of the two found no command to stand on. Solution: a fragment that declares a name and arity of the file in another section is refused with the rule that a module overloads only by arity, naming the declaration it meets, and `AlreadyDefined` stands on the second declaration.

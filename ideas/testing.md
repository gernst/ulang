# Testing

1. **The tests with formals start from the smallest values only**: they run on the enumerated values of their formals (`enumerated_tests` of `ulang/test/generate.u`), so the calls that the examples of the functions under test document are not among their cases, and they run after the other tests of their module, not in the order of the source. Solution: the examples of the functions that the test calls as its first cases, and the runners in the order of the source. They give the evidence "tested" of Verification 2.
2. **The tests of lemmas are limited**: a counterexample is a string in quotes inside `uncaught exception Falsified("…")`, with the escapes of the `Str` formals; type parameters are only `Int`; the depth is chosen once, so a lemma over two lists sees lists of at most 2 elements, and one whose assumptions small values do not meet, `requires n > 100`, is `Vacuous`; and abstract types are not enumerated. Solution: the runners print `Falsified` of `ulang/test/enumerate/enumerate.u` as `falsified for x = 1, …`, in both backends, which takes a design of its own: the runners see only the text of the uncaught value, a builtin procedure that fails a test with a message belongs to the library, whose builtin sections alone declare them, the C runtime does not know the descriptors of the types of the enumeration, which programs without tests lack, and a `show` of `TestFailure` that printed the text would not parse back; abstract types enumerated by their exported constructions, from the examples of their functions or a declared enumeration; and random values beyond the depth, by a seed in the name of the test, when a lemma needs larger cases.
3. **Examples are seen only partly outside the source**: the hover of `ulang.sh hints` shows the examples of the declarations of the main module only, since imports drop the examples of the modules they load (`without_tests_of` of `ulang/parse/parse.u`), so a use of `gcd` of the prelude shows none; and only the interpreted runner reports a failed example by its call, `gcd(12, 18) gave 3, expected 6` (`example_failure` of `ulang/test/runner.u`), while the compiled runner, `u_run_tests` of `ulang/runtime/ulang.c`, prints `assertion failed: 3 != 6`, since the text of the call is formatted from the checked test, which compiled code does not keep. Solution: the imports keep the examples of their export sections, without generating their tests, for the hover; and the test of an example asserts by a statement that knows its call, whose text the checker writes into it (by the notation of its module, which `check_package` of `ulang/check/check.u` would take from the source), so that both backends print it.
6. **The tests of lemmas over functions that do not terminate grow memory without bound** under the `ulimit -s unlimited` of `ulang.sh`, which once killed a session, so samples run under `ulimit -v` and `timeout` by hand. `check --test --timeout <seconds>` limits the time of each test in the interpreter, but not its memory, which a deep recursion fills before the limit, and the binaries of `compile --tests` take no limit. Solution: a limit of memory, by the maximal heap of the collector (`GC_set_max_heap_size`) and the depth of the stack, whose failure names the test, and both limits in the runner of the compiled tests (`u_run_tests` of `ulang/runtime/ulang.c`).
7. **Each unit expands the packages that it shares with other units again**: `check_unit` of `ulang/ulang.u` loads the interpreter of each unit once, `load(q)` of `ulang/run/run.u`, `defs_of(expanded(q))`, and the prover once, `prover_of` of `ulang/test/runner.u`, whose `symbols_of` of `ulang/prove/translate.u` expands the program too, each over all commands of the checked program, those of the library and the imports included, which the units before have expanded already. Solution: a cache of the expanded commands and the `Defs` of each package that checks without errors, as `Checks` of `ulang/check/check.u` keeps the checked ones, which takes a redesign: the checked program holds its commands as one list, without the package of each, `defs_of` would merge the `Defs` of the packages, the expansion of a package takes the pattern aliases of the whole program (`aliases_of` of `ulang/expand/expand.u`), of which it needs only those of the packages that it sees, and the ranks of the symbols of the prover are the positions of the commands in the whole program.
